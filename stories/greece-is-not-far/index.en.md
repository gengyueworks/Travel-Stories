# Greece Is Not Far: If You Want to Go, You Can Get There. Dreams Are Not Far: Take a Step and You Draw Closer.

*Geng Yue · Travel Stories*

![Greece](images/003.jpg)

Greece.

The first time I saw the book *My Heart Lingers in the Aegean*, the turquoise sea and sky on its cover struck me instantly. I felt I had found something I truly loved.

I was in high school then. Between classes, I would flip through the book again and again — a teenager dreaming of the vast world beyond. That book stayed with me for seven years. It came from Justin, a Taiwanese engineer.

The pure white walls, the cobalt blue window frames, a single bright wild rose, a slightly mysterious black cat — I remember them vividly. Back then, gazing from afar, traveling to Greece felt like the biggest dream of all.

When I went to university, it was the only travel book I brought with me. My roommate Mengmeng flipped through it and said, "I have to go here." Three years ago, I received a WeChat message from her — photos of Santorini. She said, "I'm in Greece, on my honeymoon. Because of that book, the wish I made came true."

Blue, so pure. White, so transparent.

Maugham once wrote something to the effect that some people seem born in the wrong place. They grow up somewhere, yet carry an inexplicable homesickness for a place they have never been. Perhaps it is this that drives them to travel a thousand miles to find that homeland. Until one day, they arrive somewhere and feel an immediate, wondrous sense of belonging — as if they had known it all their lives.

A good friend who lived in Greece for ten years once told me that some travelers stay on the road because they have not yet found their destination. She and her partner were lucky enough to find theirs. It was Greece.

Justin said: **Greece is not far. If you want to go, you can get there. Dreams are not far. Take a step and you draw closer...**

And I am truly about to leave for Greece.

By Yue

![Pure white building](images/017.jpg)

▲ Where do Santorini's blue and white come from? Santorini is volcanic terrain with a hot climate. The Greek government requires all houses to be painted white, because white has a cooling effect, both visually and physically. After October, Greece enters its rainy season. To preserve the pristine white, every May — before peak season — residents repaint. The white of this Art Shop is the dazzling white of fresh paint. The brushes are still right there beside it.

![Rainbow flag](images/045.jpg)

▲ Since 1978, when Gilbert Barker's rainbow flag was first used in a gay pride parade, the rainbow has become a symbol of LGBTQ+ culture. The six colors — red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple — represent life, healing, sunlight, nature and serenity, harmony, and spirit, symbolizing the diversity of the LGBTQ+ community. (Source: *2000 Handbook on Understanding Homosexuality*)

Mykonos is a vacation destination for the LGBTQ+ community. Paradise Beach and Super Paradise Beach are famous gay nude beaches. These six brushes are the sign of a bar in downtown Mykonos — a gesture of welcome.
