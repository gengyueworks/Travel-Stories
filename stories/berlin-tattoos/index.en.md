# Berlin: Behind Every Tattoo, a Secret Lies Hidden

*Geng Yue · Travel Stories*

![Berlin streets](images/002.gif)

![Tattoo design](images/003.jpg)

A friend of mine went abroad nearly two years ago and changed tremendously. What surprised me most was the tattoo on his arm.

"Why get a tattoo? That doesn't seem like you."

"I saw his designs and thought they were remarkable. I wanted to collect one, so I went."

In China, many people still associate tattoos with teenage rebellion. But the story today is about something entirely different — a full-throated, positive self-expression.

![Tattoo detail](images/004.jpg)

Today's story takes place in Berlin.

If you come to Germany, Berlin is the one place you cannot miss.

But the charm of Berlin is not something a tourist can easily grasp. You have to live there for a while. Then you will fall in love with the people. Then you will understand why everyone in this city is madly in love with Berlin.

![Berlin city](images/005.jpg)

Compared to other European cities, Berlin has one of the lowest costs of living — a powerful draw for young dreamers, students, travelers, poets, and artists. They come from everywhere, find their footing here with ease, and discover where they belong.

Berlin is inclusive, yet deeply shaped by German culture — she has a sharp personality all her own. She is not rich, but she is interesting. Much like the most magnetic young people you will ever meet.

![Berlin street scene](images/006.jpg)

The subject of today's story is Valentin Hirsch, a tattoo artist from Berlin. He studied art in Vienna. While there, whenever he drew on paper, he felt as though he were tattooing the page. He followed that instinct, and a few years later began working in this art form on skin. He has been doing it for four years now.

![Valentin Hirsch](images/007.jpg)

![Valentin at work](images/008.jpg)

Valentin's artistic grounding has driven him to continually search for and develop his own style. He does not see himself simply as a tattooist. He positions himself as an artist. His goal is to let his identity and his artistic style express themselves more and more clearly in every piece he creates.

![Tattoo design drafts](images/009.jpg)

![Tattoo work](images/010.jpg)

He combines natural elements with geometric shapes, creating a unique language that flows from the needle. This is what makes his work so distinctive.

![Nature and geometry](images/011.jpg)

![Arm tattoo](images/012.jpg)

He rejects imitation and copying. He immerses himself in study, building on years of accumulated work to practice and innovate. He places animals, symmetrical geometric patterns, and abstract forms onto his clients' arms, torsos, backs, and hands.

![Animal tattoo](images/013.jpg)

![Geometric pattern tattoo](images/014.jpg)

**People are always in awe of the things they love — it is almost like an artist's peculiar ritual.**

![Studio](images/015.jpg)

![Design drafts](images/016.jpg)

Some pianists, even when practicing alone, will put on a suit and tie. It is a gesture of respect for what they love, full of ceremony. Valentin's studio is a place like that — clean, quiet, filled mostly with his design drafts.

![Studio interior](images/017.jpg)

**A drawing can be redone if you make a mistake. If you don't like it, you can draw it again. But tattooing is a one-shot art form. Both the tattoo artist and the person choosing the tattoo must be deliberate and careful.**

![Tattooing in progress](images/018.jpg)

![Tattoo detail](images/019.jpg)

Valentin describes tattooing as something he both loves and hates.

Behind every tattoo, a secret lies hidden.

![Hidden secret](images/022.jpg)

People who come for tattoos often have their own ideas and requests. Through every tattoo, you can glimpse a story — and then, through your hands, give it new meaning. Valentin is both the interpreter and the witness of these symbols. That is what makes tattooing so captivating.

![Design sketch](images/024.jpg)

![Tattoo story](images/025.jpg)

But the preparation takes a long time — the research, the design. Then the hours of work itself can leave your back aching and sore, while you must still maintain absolute focus. Because this particular body language carries enormous weight for each person who wears it. **It represents a person's individuality and beliefs. This slightly painful, permanent mark will accompany them for the rest of their life.**

![Focused creation](images/026.jpg)

![Body art](images/027.jpg)

A tattoo is an emotional impulse. It is rational contemplation. It is a window into the soul. Choosing a tattoo is like making a promise — it will become part of your body and stay with you for a lifetime. That is why a good tattoo artist matters so deeply.

![A solemn promise](images/028.jpg)

And Valentin wants to make you that solemn promise.

**"Will you? Let me be your tattoo artist."**

A Note on Words: *Wen Shen* or *Wen Shen*?

In Chinese, the word for tattoo has two possible written forms. The original classical form, found in the *Book of Rites* ("The eastern tribes tattoo their bodies..."), uses the character 文 (*wen*), whose ancient meaning is precisely "to carve patterns into the body." The character 纹 (*wen*, with the silk radical) came later — a derivative.
