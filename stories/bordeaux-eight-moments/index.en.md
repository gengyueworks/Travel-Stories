**Five Days and Four Nights of Wine Exploration — and Eight Moments**

*Written by Qingmao*

Do you remember the first time you went to Europe —

which country, which city, what you did?

I wish I could remember it all,

but what I remember most vividly

are these eight fragments from Bordeaux.

Bordeaux

has almost everything a wine lover could want.

Aged vintages,

as lovely as a midsummer garden.

![Bordeaux vineyards](images/003.jpg)

![Bordeaux vineyards](images/004.jpg)

There's none of Paris's noise and fashion here,

but there is the elegance and French romance that flows through wine —

enough to haunt your dreams.

![Bordeaux cityscape](images/005.jpg)

🍇

Bordeaux is known as the wine capital of the world. This land is truly blessed by nature.

Bordered by the Atlantic Ocean at the 45th parallel north, it enjoys a temperate maritime climate. The Gulf Stream warms and moderates temperatures, while the vast Landes pine forest acts as a protective barrier against Atlantic winds.

![Bordeaux wine region map](images/006.gif)

Winters here are rarely harsh, springs are moist, summers are hot, and autumns are brilliantly sunny — all of which help the grapes reach optimal ripeness.

The vast territory, diverse soils, microclimates, and varied proportions of grape varieties all contribute to the richness and diversity of the wines.

![Bordeaux vineyard landscape](images/007.jpg)

**🍷 / 1**

Meeting Tongtong

at the Bordeaux Wine School

Our first night in Bordeaux, we stayed at the Hotel Normandie.

The next morning, our guide for this journey, Tongtong, was already waiting for us.

![Hotel Normandie](images/008.jpg)

Tongtong is a Beijing native, a certified instructor at the Bordeaux Wine School, living in Bordeaux.

We followed her just a few steps to the wine school, our hearts full of anticipation and excitement, as we began this journey of discovery.

![Bordeaux Wine School](images/009.gif)

Unlike any wine tasting course I'd taken before, **this time we had truly arrived in the city of Bordeaux, sitting in the tasting room of the wine school,** turning to look out the window.

In the dappled morning light, beneath swaying tree shadows, a bustling street, hurried pedestrians, and the *ding-ding-ding* of a passing tram.

**This was my first impression of Bordeaux.**

![View from the tasting room](images/010.jpg)

![Bordeaux Wine School](images/011.jpg)

My first impression of Tongtong: a warm, radiant person, nourished by the wine industry, a happy family, and an abundance of love.

Her passion for work and life, the vitality she radiates, the hearty laughter of a northern Chinese woman — it's infectious.

![Tongtong](images/012.jpg)

**The first day's course was lively and fascinating. Here are the highlights:**

√ Bordeaux history √ Geography √ Grape varieties √ Blending techniques

√ The 1855 Classification √ The AOC family (Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée)

💃

The course opened with the crowd-pleasing topic of **Eleanor of Aquitaine**.

She was a legendary woman in medieval European history.

Successively married to the King of France and then the King of England, the Duchess of Aquitaine brought all of Bordeaux as her dowry and used her influence to greatly advance the development of Bordeaux wines.

We tasted four wines. My personal favorite was the Château Barbara 2010.

![Wine tasting](images/013.jpg)

![Limestone clay soil sample](images/014.jpg)

The land of Château Barbara is characterized by limestone clay soil — Tongtong showed us a sample 👆 — so it only grows grape varieties best suited to this type of soil, such as Merlot.

The estate's vineyards sit on south-facing slopes, ensuring optimal drainage and maximum daily sun exposure for the grapes.

All these superb growing conditions make this estate one of the finest in the Canon Fronsac appellation.

![Château Barbara](images/015.jpg)

🍷 This wine offers abundant minerality, ample tannins, and a full, rich body.

On the palate: the sweetness of ripe red and black fruits;

mid-palate, the mouth fills with rich, creamy vanilla notes;

the finish is long with lingering fruit, vanilla, and a hint of licorice.

Even though this was the last wine I tasted, I could still feel its richness.

**/ 2**

The Poetry of the Cité du Vin

May Be More Beautiful Than the Wine Itself

![Cité du Vin exterior](images/019.jpg)

The Cité du Vin is a special presence in Bordeaux, with a strikingly designed exterior that resembles a wine glass.

![Cité du Vin](images/020.jpg)

Stepping inside is even more breathtaking. It's a resonant, avant-garde space — this is the **Bordeaux Wine Museum**.

![Cité du Vin interior](images/021.jpg)

![Cité du Vin interior](images/022.jpg)

Wine lovers will adore this place, for it holds a universe of wine knowledge and information.

We began in the main permanent exhibition, which occupies an entire floor on the third level. It's vast, divided into multiple themed sections that present wine through the lenses of history, geography, agriculture, and culture.

The exhibits are diverse and highly interactive. Scan a code at each station and the information loads automatically.

![Interactive exhibits](images/023.jpg)

![Interactive exhibits](images/024.jpg)

I scanned several wine regions I was curious about and tried smelling various unfamiliar scents — some delightful, some so off-putting I wrinkled my nose and covered my face, while my companions burst out laughing.

![Immersive ceiling dome](images/025.jpg)

One of my favorite immersive experiences was lying back on a circular sofa, gazing up at medieval paintings rotating overhead, while the audio guide recited poetry into my ears.

![Immersive ceiling dome](images/026.jpg)

You couldn't quite tell where the poems came from, but they harmonized perfectly with the images before your eyes. Deeply relaxing.

I quietly jotted down some of the poetry that caught my ear:

**"Drinking and the Drunken Prison"**

**/ 3**

Running and Spinning

on the Miroir d'Eau

![Miroir d'Eau](images/030.jpg)

We passed through crowds before open-air cafés, pigeons taking flight around us.

I kept getting drawn in by the stone carvings on the corners of 18th-century buildings, the colorful doors and windows, even the weathered fountains. I had to remind myself to slow down — I was walking through a UNESCO World Heritage site, in intimate contact with the city of Bordeaux.

When the water mist rose from the Miroir d'Eau, Bordeaux's iconic landmark, we played with even more abandon than the children.

![Water mist at the Miroir d'Eau](images/031.jpg)

The Miroir d'Eau gets its name from the thin layer of water that creates a "mirror of the sky" effect. Every so often, mist sprays up from the ground. When it stops, a shallow sheet of water remains, transforming the square into a mirror, reflecting everything:

![Reflections in the Miroir d'Eau](images/032.jpg)

Ancient and modern buildings reflected on the water's surface, old and new side by side. Trams gliding slowly past. Children at play. Us, running through the mist, our laughter ringing like bells, rising and falling.

Nearby, a barrel-rolling contest was warming up. Young street dancers played upbeat music to one side. People enjoyed the summer coolness at the mist's edge. You couldn't help but melt into the joyful atmosphere of the locals — this was everyone's playground.

![Miroir d'Eau](images/033.jpg)

/ **5**

"Lily Ducourt"

Tastes Like Love

Our first stop on the château tour was Vignobles Ducourt. On the way, Tongtong asked, "Do you know who Lily is?"

Before we could answer, she told us: Lily is a Chengdu girl, mentioned in Da Bing's book, a celebrity in Bordeaux. Our host was Jonathan Ducourt — Chinese name Jiu Jiu — the young heir of the estate, and Lily's husband.

![Vignobles Ducourt](images/050.jpg)

![Vignobles Ducourt](images/051.jpg)

Jiu Jiu explained the history of the Ducourt family — their crest is the crocodile — in the small village of Ladaux, southeast of Bordeaux. He is the sixth-generation heir. The younger generation follows in the footsteps of those who came before, their passion for wine transformed into professionalism and dedication, their craft refined and their business expanded through generations.

![Vignobles Ducourt](images/052.jpg)

![Vignobles Ducourt](images/053.jpg)

Curious about what "ingredients" a Chinese girl marrying a Bordeaux winery owner might bring to the wine, I followed Jiu Jiu attentively as he led us through the estate.

We visited the vineyards, the fermentation area, their offices and tasting room, and finally the family château and its "back garden." We were utterly enchanted by the breathtaking scenery. When Jiu Jiu spoke of Lily, his face was full of adoration.

![Roses in the vineyard](images/054.jpg)

🌹 In their vineyard, we noticed a rose bush planted at the head of every row of vines.

We learned that the health of the roses directly reflects the health of the grapevines — they serve as an early warning system for the vineyard.

![Lily Ducourt wine](images/055.jpg)

In the tasting room, Jiu Jiu opened a bottle of "Lily Ducourt." He explained that to win the heart of his goddess Lily, he named this wine after her. The label features a photograph of the two of them kissing. Their wedding in France would be in August, and this wine, as a wedding banquet special, would be sold in China.

Isn't this exactly the kind of love we dream of? A Chinese girl with dreams comes to Bordeaux, finds true love in a foreign land, runs a beautiful château together, and has a wine named after her, sold on the market, spreading their happiness. Never mind — let's drink this toast to them and wish the lovers a lifetime together.

![Lily Ducourt wine](images/056.jpg)

Let me describe this love-infused Lily Ducourt:

🍷 The color is a deep ruby red, with purple reflections that echo the wine's purple packaging. On the nose: an enchanting berry aroma, then deeper notes of toasted oak and vanilla.

On the palate: rich fruit, soft and fine tannins, a long, lingering finish. A wine to be enjoyed anytime, paired with cheese, fruit, or light bites.

How beautiful is Saint-Émilion?

For me, Saint-Émilion is the kind of place where, the moment you hear its name, you think not only of its vineyards and wines —

but of church bells ringing, wine shops along cobblestone lanes, cafés, and the views of half the town from different elevated platforms.

![Saint-Émilion](images/060.jpg)

We stood outside the cloister of the monastery. The weathered walls slowed our steps. I asked Tongtong: Are the stones of this monastery older than the Western Wall in Jerusalem?

![Saint-Émilion monastery](images/061.jpg)

Tongtong said they were from the same period, closely tied to the religious beliefs of the time.

We avoided the crowded main streets and slipped into the side alleys. Peering over walls, we saw that every home had a small garden, each tended differently but all exquisitely kept. The depth of wine culture in this region is something you can feel through the streets, in the very air.

The Artisan Spirit in Wine

The Unforgettable Château des Graviers

Arriving at the gates of Château des Graviers in the Margaux region, we were immediately drawn to a beautiful tree, its branches full of gentle blossoms still glistening with morning dew.

![Château des Graviers](images/062.jpg)

![Château des Graviers](images/063.jpg)

This château didn't have the grand turrets or lush gardens of other estates. It felt more like an ordinary home — quiet, unpretentious. In the courtyard, we saw many medicinal herbs and even poppies. A dog rose and wagged its tail at the sight of visitors.

Our host was Christophe Landry of Château des Graviers. What stayed with me was not only his practice of biodynamic viticulture in the vineyard but also his belief in the power of music — he regularly plays Buddhist chants for the wine aging in oak barrels and clay amphorae.

![Christophe Landry](images/064.jpg)

![Oak barrels](images/065.jpg)

![Clay amphorae](images/066.jpg)

We noticed they were using charmingly shaped clay amphorae, some with naturally darkened marks.

He explained that in their winemaking process, some of their wines use no sulfur dioxide as an antioxidant. Fermenting in clay amphorae follows traditional methods. Though the wine risks oxidation, they submerge the amphorae in water to control oxygen ingress. Over time, the vessels develop these marks.

![Clay amphorae](images/067.jpg)

![Vineyard](images/068.jpg)

![Vineyard](images/069.jpg)

![Vineyard](images/070.jpg)

He then took us into their vineyard. It was my first time walking through a vineyard dotted with wild poppies. He pointed out various herbs, telling us their names and explaining their purpose beneath the vines: retaining soil moisture, repelling pests. The biodynamic drama unfolding here was truly remarkable.

![Vineyard with poppies](images/071.jpg)

**These details are the essence of a winemaker's craft.** Every step is designed to minimize any loss of quality during the winemaking process. After tasting, we all wanted to buy their wine. These well-cared-for wines showed great elegance, with clearly distinguishable flavors.

/ 8

A Tipsy Farewell at Château Guiraud

A Toast to Our Wonderful Journey

As we walked from the parking lot toward the main entrance, we were awestruck by the blue sky, white clouds, endless vineyards, and gardens in full bloom.

A plane-tree-lined avenue wound gently into the distance, disappearing from sight. The undulating land stretched toward the distant spire of the village of Sauternes.

![Château Guiraud](images/072.jpg)

Here were gardens, a stone château with weathered walls, and the LA CHAPELLE restaurant capable of hosting several hundred guests. This was our final stop in Bordeaux — Château Guiraud in the Sauternes appellation, classified as a Premier Cru in the 1855 Classification, and one of the estates we had most eagerly anticipated.

**The entire experience of visiting and dining at Château Guiraud made me fall more and more in love with Bordeaux.**

![Château Guiraud](images/073.jpg)

- Perhaps it was seeing the estate's caring owners **building a hotel for insects**, with wild poppies blooming brilliantly in the sunlight;

- Perhaps it was touring the state-of-the-art cellars and tasting room, and seeing the open kitchen in the restaurant, **mouthwatering dishes** prepared right before our eyes;

- Perhaps it was the moment we opened a chilled bottle of Sauternes just as the kitchen brought out a plate of **prosciutto with avocado**;

- Or perhaps it was when we were chatting with the lady of the house about **her love story with the young heir**, and just then, the heir himself walked over to greet us.

**🍷 Yue's Words:**

I met Qingmao in Xiamen. Those days, our moods were beautiful.

**Her home is like a tropical botanical garden.** Sunlight streamed through during the day, casting light on the layered palm trees, green leaves, dust motes dancing in the sunbeams. Everything was just as it should be. I sat typing in the fragrance of osmanthus.

At night, we drank wine amid the scents of rosemary and cinnamon. "Don't laugh at me for drinking too much — the moonlight is coming to warm the wine."

![Qingmao's home](images/080.jpg)

We talked endlessly, never laughed enough. **Qingmao struck me as a plant, growing toward the light.** She too loves to travel, chasing the stars.

Besides running her "Tree Inn" guesthouse, she is deeply passionate about wine and taking wine courses. One evening, tipsy, we happened to talk about my wine trip to Bordeaux. She said, "For someone who loves wine, do you know how lucky you are? **It's so enviable.**"

![Qingmao](images/081.jpg)

I said, "I hope that everyone with dreams can realize them. If the opportunity arises, I would recommend **someone who truly loves wine to go to the place of their dreams and fulfill their wish.** :)"

And then, this journey of discovery truly came to be.

Perhaps my wine mission is simply to help everyone discover a larger world.
