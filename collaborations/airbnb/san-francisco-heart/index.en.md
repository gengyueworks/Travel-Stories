![San Francisco](images/003.gif)

For me, San Francisco was love at first sight.

Sitting in the car, I looked up — sunlight flooding the city. The light poured into my eyes, and my heart skipped a beat. That was the very first moment I saw San Francisco.

But back then, my vision was still narrow, my understanding still green. After I had come to know the character of countless cities, after I had returned here again and again, I never imagined that **my heart would still skip for it, just as it did the first time.**

In San Francisco, this time, I experienced seven moments of "**Encounter, Unimaginable**" — seven moments when my heart cheered.

**Unimaginable | To travel with kindred spirits**

My usual mode of travel in a foreign land is: "One person becomes an army — undiscouraged, with a calling, in love with freedom."

But **to travel with kindred spirits is truly a joy beyond words** — something you can encounter but never seek. I never imagined we would achieve it this time, with such ease.

![Travel companions](images/008.jpg)

**"Not every journey brings you interesting friends. After all, fascinating souls are one in ten thousand."** Those were Lin's words.

Lin and Ben were my two lovely travel companions in San Francisco. One an architect, the other a jewelry designer — interesting, talented people.

We met through Airbnb. Even on the road, they knew how to live well — every day, they would go to the nearby supermarket for fresh vegetables and ribs, and cook in the kitchen.

![Cooking in the kitchen](images/009.jpg)

![Food](images/010.jpg)

![The dining table](images/011.jpg)

![Eating together](images/012.jpg)

They even made a full Chinese dinner for my San Francisco host, Leslie. That night, drinking together, Lin sang the Chinese "Toast Song," and our host sang an English song. We laughed again and again. Raising our glasses, with dear friends drinking together, four figures against the light — joy untainted and pure.

![The colors of San Francisco](images/007.jpg)

**Unimaginable | A lawyer host with a sense of humor**

My Airbnb host Leslie, a San Francisco native, is a very cool American lawyer.

I had always imagined lawyers to be serious, possessed of formidable reasoning and sharp presence. But this host was hilarious, warm, and utterly endearing. She also had a German Shepherd named Shanti — enormous in size, imposing in appearance, but in personality, a sensible and well-behaved girl.

![Host Leslie and her dog](images/013.jpg)

![Shanti](images/014.jpg)

We shared a natural, similar sense of humor — the same funny bone. Our conversations were like a comedy duo act. One line after another, no matter what we talked about, north or south, we could always catch each other's cues. Every exchange was a trail of laughter.

![Leslie](images/015.jpg)

I chose this house because, when I was searching, I read the last line of the listing: "**Welcome to the Best City in the World.**"

It inexplicably moved me. It made me want to say hello to the host — and then this adventure began.

**Unimaginable | Beauty happens in unexpected places**

While having coffee at Mission Pie, Lin and Ben showed us a renovation project they had done in Shenzhen — splashing colorful paint onto the walls of dilapidated residential buildings, bringing them back to life. And they said: "**Beauty happens in unexpected places.**"

The host thought it was so cool. Excited, she said, "Can we go splash some paint around right now?"

![Graffiti wall](images/027.jpg)

![Mural](images/028.jpg)

![Street art](images/029.jpg)

The Mission District, near the host's home, has the most mural walls in San Francisco. Street artists of all kinds come here to create freely.

First, turn into Clarion Alley — then head to The Women's Building, and when you come out of the 24th Street BART station, you'll see all kinds of graffiti walls — stunning at every turn.

![Graffiti](images/030.jpg)

![Mural wall](images/031.jpg)

![Street art 2](images/032.jpg)

![Graffiti wall 2](images/033.jpg)

To make sure we didn't miss the best of the graffiti culture, we joined an experience hosted by a photographer named Kyle.

Meeting Kyle became a major turning point in this San Francisco journey. We were in the same field and got along wonderfully. He was about to leave for Iceland and said, "**I have a carnival photographer pass — it's a shame I can't use it. Yue, I'm giving it to you!**"

![Kyle](images/034.gif)

![Kyle taking photos](images/035.gif)

![Kyle explaining](images/036.gif)

Kyle seemed modest and unassuming, but he could speak fluently and offhandedly about the history and culture of graffiti and architecture. All along the way, he humbly praised my travel experience and so on.

But when I happened to glance at his business card, I saw that his email domain was Harvard.edu. It turned out that he was actually an extraordinary travel photographer and teacher who had photographed in over a hundred countries!

![Kyle's business card](images/037.jpg)

I never imagined I would meet such a deeply accomplished senior! And I even received a precious carnival pass.

**I began to realize that San Francisco is a "city of miracles."** Who knows whom you might meet, what stories might unfold?

![San Francisco street scene](images/026.jpg)

**Unimaginable | Lost my passport, then healed by a meditation**

Next came an enormous "unimaginable" — I discovered I had lost my passport!

A passport is the most important thing in travel. When I confirmed this terrible news, my mood plummeted to rock bottom.

I was ready to stay in the car and cry for a while — but the sadness lasted only three seconds.

![Sadness](images/038.gif)

The host waited for me to cry, but the moment she saw my expression, she couldn't help but laugh.

— "This is just one second in the long river of your happiness. In three or four months, you'll think nothing of it. But the joy — you'll remember that forever."

— "You give, you receive. Your moods rise and fall — like a circle. This is the principle by which the universe has always worked," she said.

![Meditation scene](images/039.jpg)

![Meditation](images/040.jpg)

In that moment, I was warmed by good energy. I got out of the car and went to join the meditation experience.

The young man leading the meditation struck a singing bowl and said, "Close your eyes. Let go of past emotions and hurts. Feel the one word that rises in your mind."

![The singing bowl](images/041.jpg)

— Me: Focus.

— Host: Gratitude.

— Lin: The past mind cannot be grasped; the present mind cannot be grasped; the future mind cannot be grasped.

— Ben: I'm so sleepy.

I never imagined that my despondent mood would vanish in an instant. Without such thoughtful travel companions, without this meditation — I know I would not have recovered nearly so fast.

**Unimaginable | I became the only Chinese photographer at the carnival**

![Carnival](images/042.jpg)

![Carnival parade](images/043.jpg)

This was a grand party that only exists in San Francisco, a parade that draws the eyes of all.

The joy floating in the wind, the bronze skin gleaming with San Francisco's unique light — it stirred every nerve in my body.

I raised my camera and ran alongside the parade. Though I was a spectator, in my heart, I felt a strange happiness — as if I had been living here for a long, long time.

![Carnival dancing](images/044.gif)

![Carnival costumes](images/045.jpg)

I had seen coverage of the carnival before, but I never imagined I would be at the 40th edition, as the only Chinese photographer. Many performers in elaborate costumes greeted me, hoping I would photograph them more, listen to their stories.

And one more unimaginable thing: the host, it turned out, was also participating in the carnival, in my favorite troupe, Viva La Diva. We recognized each other on the spot, marveling at **how small the world is, how serendipitous fate can be.**

![Host at the carnival](images/046.jpg)

![Carnival group photo](images/047.jpg)

Lin and Ben also caught several dance performances, danced together, and experienced their first-ever carnival.

**Unimaginable | We got tickets to Alcatraz**

Kyle suggested there was another very cool place worth visiting. "**Alcatraz — but the tickets are incredibly hard to get.**"

Alcatraz Island — the most sinister and terrifying prison in history, where the most dangerous felons and mafia bosses once served time or attempted escape.

Conventional wisdom says visitors need to book at least 18 days in advance to have a chance of getting on the island.

![Alcatraz](images/048.jpg)

![Alcatraz buildings](images/049.jpg)

![Alcatraz prison](images/050.jpg)

Just as we were about to give up, I never imagined — I actually managed to snatch tickets!

We caught the ferry and saw the island surrounded by seawater, isolated from the world.

A towering cliff, a lighthouse standing alone beside it, seabirds circling the lighthouse in tireless low flight.

![Alcatraz lighthouse](images/051.jpg)

![Alcatraz cell](images/052.jpg)

We walked into the former cell blocks. In those cramped spaces, the stories and passions of the prisoners were hidden — some had sketchboards, some had guitars, accordions, novels... Amid the rusted bars, we searched for tales of sin and redemption.

But on that afternoon in a place of confinement, what I felt instead was freedom. **A kind of freedom — detached from the world, standing quietly at the end of it.**

![Alcatraz from afar](images/057.jpg)

This time, I left my heart in San Francisco.

Every main street, every corner, every scent in this city — in every glance, I could see sunlight.

We drove up the hills and down the hills. Outside the window, hippie kids played, skateboarders flew past. The Pacific wind billowed our shirts. The wind settled, then rose again.

Encounter, unimaginable. In San Francisco, in the story of my journey this time, it means love and freedom.

"Your San Francisco journey is so unique — it's like a movie, one unimaginable thing after another, linked together," Kyle said.

"Though this is my city, as familiar as it gets — when I see it through your eyes, through the eyes of a traveler, I still fall in love with it all over again, rediscovering San Francisco's sparkle and shine. A perfect adventure," said my host Leslie.

"**Isn't life just like this? When you're not looking, it arranges all kinds of circumstances for you — whether shock or surprise, every one is a miracle in the present moment,**" Lin said.

Yes. Though this time, I nearly lost every material possession a traveler carries, those completely unpredictable, moving moments washed over me in wave after wave, flowing through the brilliant golden light. Joy rose like the tide.

Every one of them deserves a toast — to myself, to kindred spirits, to heaven and earth, and to time.

**San Francisco Spots Mentioned in This Article**

![Mission Pie](images/058.jpg)

**Mission Pie**

◎ Mission Pie serves savory pies with spinach, carrots, and brown rice — humble in appearance, but the ingredients come directly from farms, From Market to Table, with locally grown bouquets and jams.

Add: 2901 Mission St at 25th, San Francisco, CA 94110

Hours: Weekdays 7am–10pm / Saturday 8am–10pm / Sunday 9am–10pm

**Mission District Graffiti Walls**

◎ "San Francisco has over 1,000 murals, and the Mission District holds the vast majority of them." This area is now a gathering place for San Francisco's new-generation hippies and young artists.

◎ The rich colors make for stunning photo backdrops.

◎ Every piece of graffiti is created for different social, cultural, or contextual reasons. The most famous are the murals and the Mexican food. Many street art graffiti pieces express opinions on government and society through their themes — such as immigrant community tensions in Latin America, acute social issues, and more.

◎ After walking the streets myself, Mission has two alleys best for viewing graffiti:

1) Clarion Alley, near the 16th and Mission BART station

2) Balmy St, near the 24th and Mission BART station

3) The Women's Building — 3543 18th St #8, San Francisco, CA 94110

**Dolores Park**

◎ From the graffiti walls to Dolores Park is just a few minutes' drive. This park, in the heart of the Mission, is a wonderful place to observe the kaleidoscope of San Francisco life and experience authentic American daily life.

◎ "If you haven't whiled away time here, you're not a true San Franciscan." Most of the people living nearby are freelancers and artists, so even on a weekday afternoon, you can see them basking in the sun with nothing to do.

A few friends, snacks and wine, card games, meditation, yoga, magic tricks and juggling, guitar and singing... Here, you can easily while away an afternoon, and feel the incomparably free and easy atmosphere.

◎ Address: Dolores Park — 19th St & Dolores Street, San Francisco, CA 94114, USA

**Carnival**

◎ San Francisco's grandest local festival, once a year. Usually held in the last week of May.

◎ The parade route is mainly in the Mission District. If you want to watch, bring a small stool in advance, find a good spot along the roadside, and look for some shade.

**Alcatraz Island**

◎ Tickets: https://www.alcatrazcruises.com/

◎ Departure is by ferry from Pier 33 in San Francisco. There are different time slots throughout the day — the one you choose when purchasing your ticket is the one you take. We chose midday, spent about three hours on Alcatraz, then took the ferry back — just in time to enjoy a musical dinner by the sea near the pier.

◎ It's best to print your ferry ticket in advance, and print the proof of credit card purchase. Be sure to bring your passport.
