# Xiamen · At the Blue Island

*Geng Yue · Travel Stories*

![Blue Island sea view](images/002.gif)

1

**A Toast to Kindred Spirits**

My transformation in Xiamen — from a mere traveler to someone who "lives alongside the locals" — began with a small winter gathering.

That evening, my friend in Xiamen, the florist Oxygen, introduced me to her closest friends, Kiki and Qingmao. Kiki had prepared a home-cooked feast just for us: ginger duck, oyster omelette, fresh fish and shrimp — the kind of homestyle Minnan cooking that made me forget every tourist snack I had ever tried on Zengcuoan's streets. The steaming tomato brisket soup warmed me from the inside out, each spoonful nourishing my memory of food that winter.

![Home feast table](images/003.jpg)

![Dishes on the table](images/004.jpg)

![A lavish home-cooked meal](images/005.jpg)

We were friends forged on the road. I first met Oxygen in Taiwan, and since then, I had watched her grow with laughter and ease. That night, gathered around the dining table, we traded stories from distant places, talked about Xiamen's creative circles, and in the rising and falling of conversation and laughter, our passion for life filled the room. In that warm home feast, those joyful hours together **transformed me from a traveler into someone who tasted the flavor of home and friendship.**

That was how we began. Later, I came to realize: **these women were living a real-life version of Kinfolk. Friends. Gatherings. Food. The table. A love of life. Sharing stories. Texture. A little slower, a little more beautiful.**

2

**No Travel Hassles Here — Only Life and the Shape of Friendship**

Kiki's kitchen was a small white room under a glass roof. Inside pearl-white cabinets, she kept cups, dried flowers, and fruit. We shared many meals there.

![Kiki's white kitchen](images/006.jpg)

One morning at ten, we had brunch there: naturally leavened sourdough bread from the city, Kiki's homemade strawberry jam. A café owner from Wuhu joined us that day — she comes to stay here every year. And there were many second-time, third-time, even Nth-time guests. When they saw Kiki again, a simple "You're here" was all it took — like the reunion of old friends, easy and reassuring. And every time they met, each had become a better version of themselves.

That is the thing: when you think of a travel destination and think of the friends who live there, even if you are alone, you feel safe. Because Kiki pours her heart into everything. She says, "This place might not dazzle you at first glance, but once you're here, I will take care of you with all my heart."

![Kiki's brunch](images/007.jpg)

One morning when I woke, she made me brunch with bread, eggs, mushrooms, and strawberries.

One rainy midday, she simmered a large pot of noodles in scallion oil — old-school Xiamen breakfast, a steaming bowl so deeply satisfying, best finished with a fried dough stick from the Eighth Market, the only truly local way. I had bowl after bowl, because back in Beijing, I would never find anything this good again.

![Scallion oil noodles with fried dough stick](images/008.jpg)

One evening, I stayed in my room writing and skipped dinner. When I went to say hello, she heated up a garlic baguette and a chocolate brioche filled with chestnut paste. As I was about to carry them away, she said, "Let me make you a cup of hot tea to go with that."

![Baguette and hot tea](images/009.jpg)

Everything she cooked was delicious. She made desserts too, with an instinctive understanding of ingredients — the flavors either delicate or rich and dense. She found and created beauty in the everyday, even if it was just a cup of tea, a teacup, a spoonful of homemade jam.

![Kiki's teaware](images/010.jpg)

Her teaware was beautiful too. When we talk about the quality of life, let us first discard all plastic objects. Things made from natural materials — wood, clay — are not only used; they are touched, admired, and every encounter with them is a moment of loveliness.

![Flowers in the courtyard](images/011.jpg)

![Flowers after rain](images/012.jpg)

The flowers in Kiki's courtyard, drinking their fill of rain.

I have flown to dozens of cities around the world, lived in places with breakneck rhythms. But this time in Xiamen, I was moved by people who live this way.

3

**Beside the Sea, You Become Your Own Island**

I stayed at Kiki's guesthouse — "Blue Island" — a white house beside a blue sea, at the deepest end of Huangcuo Xitouxia in Xiamen.

![Blue Island guesthouse exterior](images/013.jpg)

![The white house](images/014.jpg)

![Blue Island courtyard](images/015.jpg)

![Blue Island interior](images/016.jpg)

![Blue Island room](images/017.jpg)

I fell in love with one room — a room with a view. It had a beautiful name: "Waltz."

I used to collect photographs of writers' desks and windows from around the world, and one of them looked very much like this. So when I stepped inside, it felt entirely familiar.

You can dwell in a clean, quiet space, the sea just a breath away, watching the horizon where sky and water meet, listening to the wind rise and the rain fall.

I imagined myself there: writing, gazing at the sea, brewing tea, passing idle hours of good time.

A line came to mind, the kind of life I once woke up longing for: *Spring flowers, autumn moon; summer breeze, winter snow. When no idle worries weigh upon the heart, any season is the best season.*

At least in those silent moments, I could feel the tenderness of this city.

At least I could taste, for a while, the "best season."

I love photographing beautiful bokeh on sunny days. I do appreciate the design sensibility of hotels — their convenience and efficiency, when I need to maintain the pace of an urbanite. But when I am on holiday, when I want to unwind, I prefer a guesthouse — for the warmth, for the human touch, for every detail made with love and care.

So many people in this world dream of owning a house that "faces the sea, with spring blossoms." But most never do it. She, who loved life by the sea, quit her job in Shanghai, returned to Xiamen, and turned that dream into reality.

![Blue Island living room](images/024.jpg)

**We are all people who do things for the sake of our own hearts, and we understand the enchantment and the hardship alike.** But when you persist, the aura becomes natural, and it draws the right people to you — like the blogger Cha Jie (who Kiki had admired for years) suddenly booking the entire place for New Year's Eve, or like me, traveling from afar, finding a kindred spirit at first meeting.

![Blue Island bedside](images/025.jpg)

![Blue Island corner](images/026.jpg)

Kiki runs this place with love. She pours her passion for life into every corner of her warm guesthouse. Her voice is always gentle, never raised — she says she has never argued with anyone in her life. Everything she touches, everything she handles, must be something she genuinely likes. She is simple-hearted, but **she has her own aesthetic, her own taste, her own sense of order.**

For the last few days, I stayed in her newly renovated house — elegant, filled with simple, naturally shaped objects.

Her social media posts said: the rooms have no unnecessary decoration, because sunlight, breeze, and the sea view are already beautiful enough. Every essential item is carefully chosen — good materials, good design — so the warmth of home and life lingers, natural and comfortable.

I volunteered to photograph her rooms. The bright, quiet visual quality of the spaces matched my own style of gentle yet resolute visual storytelling perfectly.

**We Opened Up a Xiamen Not Found in Any Travel Guide**

![Yue and Kiki in front of the tile wall at the Old Things Warehouse](images/027.jpg)

Kiki and I, standing before the tile wall at Xiamen's Old Things Warehouse.

Anyone who knows my writing knows I avoid tourist sites. This time, **following two seasoned connoisseurs of the artsy life, we opened up a Xiamen not found in any travel guide.**

Kiki and Qingmao are neighbors; their guesthouses sit side by side. The three of us grew closer and closer, as if we had known each other for years. Whenever they were free during the day, they would take me exploring Xiamen.

![Kiki and Qingmao's daily life](images/028.jpg)

Kiki and Qingmao, neighbors — the everyday rhythm of my two dear friends in Xiamen.

We wandered slowly through the Eighth Market, Xiamen's oldest wet market, circling it several times. We sat at roadside stalls eating the most local snacks, squeezed onto a round table with three groups of locals at Liangshan Dai Pai Dang, and explored many wonderful shops. They took me to Maishang Chujian for garlic bread, to Sparrow for Taiwanese braised pork rice, to Sun Dance Café for handmade egg rolls, to Hongyi Desserts for old-fashioned tangyuan. We went to the Old Things Warehouse to browse vintage objects, listened to a learned architect hold forth on grand ideas... **In our eyes, food is only good or not — never noble or humble. Friends are only sincere and interesting — never ranked by profession.**

On rainy days, we carried our laptops to Invisible Café next to Blue Island, sat writing beneath the Austin roses hanging from the glass roof, working. When the weather turned cold, we gathered around the heater, eating a little, talking a little. "It seemed we had already talked for days and nights, and still had things to say, still couldn't bear for you to leave." It felt as though I could never tire of their company. The second time we went to the little shop Sparrow for braised pork rice, it still tasted wonderful, and my heart was still light with delight.

As I tasted those foods one bite at a time, stepped into those rooms one by one, those flavors, those experiences were poured into my love for Xiamen, enriching my memory of this city.

6

**In a Gilded Afternoon, Anxiety Is Worthless Here**

Every day in Xiamen, I felt clear-headed and bright-eyed. The first few days after I moved into this guesthouse, the sun shone every day — golden light, brilliant blue sky, the firecracker flowers blooming riotously in the courtyard. One morning I woke and felt my heart, which had been clenched like a tight fist, loosen just a little.

Two years ago, I had visited Xiamen once. The impression was pleasant enough, but nothing stirred in me the desire to stay. This winter visit, however — the life I encountered, the warmth of human connection — it echoed the life I had always dreamed of, as if by unspoken agreement. Through every moment, every person, every view, it told me: **you can try. Stay. Stay a while.**

![The shimmering sea from Blue Island's window](images/036.jpg)

![Sea view from Blue Island](images/037.jpg)

The shimmering sea, seen from the window of Blue Island.

And I didn't miss my life in Beijing at all.

One day, sitting in the car with loaves of bread in our arms on the way back, we chased a pink sunset ahead of us. It was fleeting — **but it reminded me of California summers and evening glows.** On this journey, I didn't feel much like taking photographs. Many of the most beautiful moments were never captured by the lens. What stayed in my heart were the people here, and the state of mind I was in.

**Just One Turn Away**

We are always blaming our dreams, calling them nothing but flowers in a mirror. But if you would only turn around,

face the person you were at the beginning, and take one step,

perhaps you really could touch it —

even if only...

Xiamen, I love you. I never expected to find such wonderful companions in a strange place. And time — time is now, this moment, all the adventures we have lived through. **I believe I will return. I will write here. I will fall asleep and wake to the fierce tenderness of the waves. I will set out again from here.** If I am ever a little downcast, or if I set off anew, I will still be willfully full of hope.
