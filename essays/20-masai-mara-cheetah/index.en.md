Having grown up in Xinjiang, I felt as though Africa might be my second homeland. I had read *Out of Africa*, *Thirty Thousand Miles Across Africa*, *West with the Night* — they stirred in me a longing that felt unreachable, a faraway place that so many explorers yearn for but dare not challenge.
(I left this red-and-green shawl behind on the Masai Mara — in every place I love, I leave a small keepsake.)

![On the grasslands](images/008.jpg)

I arrived on this distant, dreamed-of land carrying impressions of acacia trees, the snows of Kilimanjaro, Karen Blixen's love story, Hemingway's hunting expeditions.

![The land of dreams](images/009.jpg)

The moment the jeep drove into the grasslands, my heart soared. The clouds hung low here, the view stretched wide — a hundred miles of flat, open, rolling savannah.

Shadow said, **The Masai Mara is the reason I fell in love with Kenya.** I grew even more eager to see those extraordinary, precious wild animals.

![Jeep on the grasslands](images/010.jpg)

By then, I had already seen the breathtaking sunrise over Kilimanjaro's snows at dawn, and the dazzling, star-filled night sky.

But still, I was struck by the minimalist beauty of the Masai Mara — the pure blue sky, the vast, undulating grasslands, and the solitary acacia trees standing alone.

![Acacia tree](images/011.jpg)

In such a remote place, I had unexpectedly found a long-lost sense of pastoral poetry and romance, echoing across the distance.

![Grasslands landscape](images/012.jpg)

![Clouds over the grasslands](images/013.jpg)

The Maasai set fire to the dry, withered grass, turning the golden fields barren and charred. Smoke blurred the view as our jeep drove through it, stinging our eyes shut.

![Burning grasslands](images/014.jpg)

![Smoke over the grasslands](images/015.jpg)

Every jeep has its own one-of-a-kind Safari journey across the grasslands.

I felt deeply that everyone who makes it here is fortunate, blessed.

![Safari journey](images/016.jpg)

Starting with this giraffe, the animals I had seen in *National Geographic*, *Animal World*, and every African wildlife documentary began to appear, one by one.

![Giraffe](images/017.jpg)

Many people come to Kenya for the animals — to see the "Big Five" (African elephant, rhinoceros, buffalo, lion, leopard), the five treasures also printed on the South African rand.

![The Big Five](images/018.jpg)

![African elephant](images/019.jpg)

When the lions — the overlords of the grasslands — began to appear, childhood memories of animation came rushing back: Simba, the wild roar across the savannah.

![Lion pride](images/020.jpg)

![Lion](images/021.jpg)

An African buffalo lay in the grass. A vegetarian, yes — but in truth one of the most dangerous beasts in Africa. Our jeep paused for only a few seconds before speeding away.

![African buffalo](images/022.jpg)

Hippos lay motionless in the water. I had always thought of them as gentle and endearing, but they are actually fierce-tempered, convinced their territory is inviolable, and each year they kill several people who trespass into their domain.

![Hippos](images/023.jpg)

After learning their true nature, when one suddenly opened its mouth right in front of me, I was genuinely startled.

![Hippo opening its mouth](images/024.jpg)

Our driver was already a veteran of the grasslands. The crocodiles, nearly identical in color to the mud and water, lay silently in wait. But as the jeep passed the river, the driver suddenly stopped, pointed at a patch of muck, and said: go ahead, shoot. The two most dangerous animals you can encounter by the water's edge — hippos and crocodiles.

![Crocodile](images/025.jpg)

![Crocodile in the river](images/026.jpg)

Zebras were a common sight on the grasslands. Watching their swaying rumps, I hummed Song Dongye's song: "Zebra, zebra, you've returned to your home." They are sensitive creatures — the moment they sense our gaze, they turn their heads back warily. Their black-and-white stripes are truly beautiful.

![Zebra](images/027.jpg)

Though we constantly reminded ourselves that this was a savannah ruled by survival of the fittest, every time I turned my head and saw a giraffe drifting by in unhurried grace, I could still believe that this was a paradise and playground for animals.

![Graceful giraffe](images/028.jpg)

![Herd of giraffes](images/029.jpg)

![Giraffe close-up](images/030.jpg)

Though Kenya's most sought-after spectacle is the Great Migration in July and August, visiting in April, we still saw every animal we had hoped to see. Truly, this is a place suited for travel all year round.

![The Great Migration](images/031.jpg)

![Animals on the grasslands](images/032.jpg)

I recalled what someone once said: on the African savannah, the humans driving jeeps to chase animals are like ants, while the animals are the true masters. Every day, unpredictable dramas unfold here.

![The chase on the grasslands](images/033.jpg)

There seemed to be a disturbance on the grasslands. We spotted antelope walking through the tall grass in the distance — but closer by, a leopard was watching them in silence.

![Leopard and antelope](images/034.jpg)

This was a moment of standoff on the savannah.

![Standoff](images/035.jpg)

When I saw the fastest land animal on Earth — the cheetah — I could hardly believe my eyes. It was supposed to be a ferocious creature, yet there it lay before me, looking almost adorably goofy.

![Adorable cheetah](images/036.jpg)

Even a little languid. In an instant, I fell in love with it as if it were a pet.

![Languid cheetah](images/037.jpg)

But when the cheetah rose and began to move, there was a captivating beauty to it — and a hint of aggression.

![Cheetah walking](images/038.jpg)

The scene of a cheetah hunt was unfolding right before my eyes! I had only ever seen cheetahs chasing antelope on television. I desperately hoped the antelope would escape!

![Cheetah chasing](images/039.jpg)

A theoretical analysis: the outcome of this chase seemed almost predictable. The cheetah is the fastest land animal, averaging over 90 km/h, reaching a top speed of 110 km/h, while the antelope can only manage 90 km/h.

But the cheetah can only sustain its top speed for three to four minutes. The antelope, however, is a long-distance champion — it can run at 90 km/h for over ten minutes. If it can evade capture in those first few minutes, it can survive.
I apologize for my poor math, but the point is: the antelope actually had a chance!

![Cheetah in pursuit](images/040.jpg)

Our jeep chased after the cheetahs. This Thomson's gazelle was being pursued relentlessly by three large cheetahs.

![Three cheetahs in pursuit](images/041.jpg)

In the instant just before contact, I held my breath.

![Almost upon it](images/042.jpg)

The young gazelle stumbled and fell, lying right before the cheetahs!

![Gazelle falls](images/043.jpg)

Several cheetahs pinned the young gazelle down. I thought they were about to start eating, that a bloody scene was about to unfold.

![Cheetahs pin the gazelle](images/044.jpg)

Then, I saw a look of surprise on the cheetahs' faces. By then, I thought the young gazelle was already dead.

![Surprised cheetah](images/045.jpg)

Who would have guessed — it suddenly stood up, seized the moment when the cheetahs were distracted, and began to flee!

![Gazelle fleeing](images/046.jpg)

The cheetahs began to chase again... It turned out they had never intended to actually eat the young gazelle. They were simply teaching the cubs how to hunt, and the young gazelle had happened to become their "practice target."

![Chasing again](images/047.jpg)

![The chase scene](images/048.jpg)

![Cheetahs practicing](images/049.jpg)

At that moment, the voice in my head was screaming: let that antelope go!!

![Let the antelope go](images/050.jpg)

On the Masai Mara, every dream I had ever had about Africa came true. When you see with your own eyes the prides of lions, the cheetahs, the elephants, giraffes, buffalo, wildebeest, zebras — you know that falling in love with Africa is simply the easiest thing in the world.
