**Two Films**

The narration of *Loving Lanka* comes from a speech by Alan Watts, a British philosopher deeply versed in Buddhism.

*"We are accustomed to think of ourselves as puppets of the past, and we dream of the strings behind us, but I want to turn the whole thing completely upside down."*

The film has no subtitles. The narration is beautiful:

"The flowers bloom brightly, the stars are brilliant, the earth gives off fragrance, raindrops murmur, everything shines once more.

Once again, you see another side of the world.

What you love. Men, women."

![Loving Lanka film clip](images/005.gif)

Sebastian embraces the philosophy of **living in the present.** "I would say, the past is a result of the present."

That is why the film is filled with moments of **walking backward.** It is what makes the video so unique.

*Beast*, on the other hand, uses color grading, high-speed cinematography, smoke, and dust to reveal the singular beauty of skateboarding. Every single frame, taken alone, is a **stunning work of photographic art.**

**On Travel: "If you really want to improve at something, get out there and spend time on it."**

By October 2014, heavy workloads and commercial filmmaking had left him suffocating. To break free from the grind, he quit everything, proposed to the woman he loved, and brought her to Sri Lanka.

Sebastian shot over 60 hours of footage. In the end, he edited it down to a short film of less than three minutes.

For a creator, that is a kind of beautiful agony.

He wanted his video to feel like dancing — because a dance is never boring. **Every second brings a new surprise.**

To achieve that effect, he often imitated dance moves to spark inspiration.

![Sebastian and his friends](images/016.jpg)

**> Sebastian and his friends <**

His friends describe him as **insane.**

"After he told me 'this is the final version' ten times, I finally saw one of the actual final versions."

**"I love real moments. Stories made from real people and real lives.**

I want to be inside those stories and moments. My only goal is to make you feel those moments in the video."

**On Skateboarding: "The first time I saw a skateboarder, I was only six years old. I believed it had to be magic that made him fly."**

![Young skateboarder](images/018.gif)

He has made many skateboarding films, like the *Beast* series, which he calls his "journey to reclaim a dream."

![Beast series clip](images/019.gif)

For Sebastian, the skateboard is not just a subject — it is also his camera rig. Every shot in *Loving Lanka* was captured by Sebastian himself, riding his skateboard and spinning the camera. No camera dolly. No complex post-production.
