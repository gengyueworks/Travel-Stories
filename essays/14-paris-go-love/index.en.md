**An Airbnb in the Heart of Montmartre**

The host waited for me at the door, handed me the keys, and said, "My dear, you have chosen so well. Our home is at the very 'heart' of Montmartre."

After taking in the view of half of Paris from above, the moment I stepped out the door, I began exploring this deeply artistic neighborhood.

**Café des Deux Moulins**

A five-minute stroll from the door brought me to Café des Deux Moulins —

the café where Amélie works in the film.

I ordered the signature crème brûlée.

![Café des Deux Moulins](images/037.jpg)

![Café des Deux Moulins](images/038.jpg)

**Cinema Studio 28**

Walking further from the café, you pass the famous Moulin Rouge.

A little further up the hill and you reach my favorite secret spot.

Sitting in red velvet seats, watching a French arthouse film.

I spent one of the most enchanting Parisian evenings here.

It opened in 1928, hailed by Jean Cocteau as "the masterpiece among cinemas, the cinema among masterpieces."

**Paris is A Gift**

My love for Amélie brings me back again to

that beloved poem by Alfred D'Souza:

Love like you've never been hurt.

Dance like nobody's watching.

Sing like nobody's listening.

Work like you don't need the money.

Live like it's heaven on earth.

— Alfred D'Souza

I bought a few postcards to send to you, far away,

wanting to share the beauty of this day with you, wanting to tell you:

**When you are young, you must go to Paris at least once.**
