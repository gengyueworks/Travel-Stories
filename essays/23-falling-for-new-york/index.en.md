**Why I Love New York**

![Reasons to love New York](images/003.jpg)

![Summer days in New York](images/004.jpg)

The cusp of spring and summer is when you're most likely to fall for New York, almost without meaning to.

Under a boundless blue sky, you can spend an entire day doing nothing at all. Summer dusks arrive with their own built-in filter — the sky ablaze — and you can let a shopping stroll stretch time into something slow and sweet.

Whether you're wandering through Central Park, admiring beautiful bodies in motion, or weaving through a dozen languages along the High Line —

![Leisurely moments in the park](images/005.jpg)

New York always has a pocket of calm — a park, a bar — where a weary spirit can finally exhale.

Step out of the office, sit down among tourists, sip a coffee, catch an open-air film, chat with a good friend over drinks, and race the clock to catch the subway back to Queens or Newport. **That's a good day.**

**Museums**

![Museum](images/006.jpg)

![Museum](images/007.jpg)

I know it's a cliché to talk about museums, but I love curling up at home with a cup of tea and Shen Xincheng's book *No One Is a Stranger in New York*. Reading it while museum-hopping is its own kind of pleasure.

Honestly, you could just order it online back home and have it delivered to your door — no need to buy a plane ticket or fight jet lag to stand before the endless masterpieces here. The book alone brings the city's museum history and culture vividly to life.

![No One Is a Stranger in New York](images/008.jpg)

![MoMA](images/009.jpg)

I also love those quiet evenings when I don't have to work late. With my MoMA membership, I slip in after the tourists have gone, navigating familiar halls to stand before Monet and Van Gogh.

It feels like something out of *Sex and the City*, but it's really just an ordinary, unremarkable day. And then you catch yourself thinking — see? **This city still has things worth staying for.**

**Culture and Cuisine**

In New York, culture and food belong in the same sentence. They tell the story of where you come from, which neighborhood you frequent, how you dress, and who you eat with.

East Village, West Village, Midtown, Downtown, Elmhurst, Flushing — if I could show you my Google Map, the pins marking restaurants I've tried and restaurants I want to try are so dense I can barely believe it myself. The ten pounds I've gained are a testament to all of it. New Yorkers eat out at a rate that borders on the extreme.

![New York cuisine](images/012.jpg)

![New York cuisine](images/013.jpg)

![New York cuisine](images/014.jpg)

The holy trinity of French, Italian, and American classics. Chinese food. Korean kimchi tofu stew. Japanese ramen and sushi. Thai. Mexican tacos. Vietnamese pho. Turkish kebabs. Danish cuisine. It's as if someone crammed the entire world map onto a single crowded street.

Michelin stars or not, every place shines with its own light — and that alone is enough.

And of course, biting into a perfectly juicy, authentic pan-fried pork bun, diving into a deeply flavorful mala xiang guo or hot pot, waiting in line for dim sum with endless variety — these moments of cultural recognition are the purest joy. Manhattan or Flushing, the relentless wave of high-standard Chinese food has thoroughly defeated Americanized Chinese takeout, and the Chinese stomachs of New York have never been happier.

**Secret Tips**

![The Press Lounge rooftop bar](images/015.jpg)

- It's summer. Go to a rooftop bar. The Press Lounge is a favorite.
- Grab a beer and some cheese from the Whole Foods at Bryant Park, find a window seat, and drink in the green and the stream of passersby.
- Stop on the street and take in the confidence and humor of a street performer — a tap dance, an appreciative crowd. Take a photo, but also leave a tip, whatever you can spare. It's about respect for the art.

![Street performer](images/016.jpg)

![Street performer](images/017.jpg)

- Catch Woody Allen's band at Cafe Carlyle and savor an elegance and sophistication that dates back to 1955.

![Cafe Carlyle](images/018.jpg)

- If the timing is right, go to the Jazz Age Lawn Party on Governors Island. Dress up like someone from a century ago, dance, picnic, and soak in the sunshine and music.

**A Conversation with Yuan Wang**

![Yuan Wang](images/019.jpg)

Yuan Wang once worked on Fifth Avenue in Manhattan, in the glamorous world of fashion. Between the glitter and the high pressure, she spent every vacation traveling — over twenty countries, more than sixty cities. She has tracked lions and cheetahs across the African savanna and climbed Arctic glaciers to witness the staggering reality of melting ice. Because she loves food and believes it is a conduit for human connection, she made a dramatic career pivot, spending years immersing herself in pastry and baking arts, eventually landing a position at the three-Michelin-star Jean-Georges under Chef Joseph Murphy. Now she is crossing into yet another field, forging new connections.

**What's your most vivid memory from your first visit to New York, ten years ago?**

![Central Park](images/020.jpg)

- Ten years ago, there was no Google Maps. I brought a travel guide from China, flipped to the page on Central Park, and promptly got lost, asking strangers for directions.

I vividly remember chatting with a white man out for a walk. He told me, with a certain pride, that he had never once left Manhattan in his entire life — not to Brooklyn, not to Queens. He believed he was already living in the best place on earth.

Back then, I sternly told myself: if I ever come to live in New York, I must never become someone like that. But after a few years here, I suddenly understood. Perhaps I should thank this city for teaching me to be more tolerant, more respectful of different cultures — this distinctly New Yorker brand of self-contained indifference.

**That's exactly what I mean by freedom. The freedom to choose who you want to be. Why worry about who really cares who you are? This city is so busy, so many people rushing past.**

**Diversity and Freedom**

- In New York, aside from your boss and your clients, no one really seems to care how good your English is. This immense tolerance is both comforting and disorienting. (That IELTS speaking score of 7 I worked so hard for? Turns out it doesn't matter much. Just remember to smile, nod, and be friendly.)

On the subway, someone is always puzzling over real-time route changes, asking in accented English — or a language you can't understand — whether they're heading the right direction. If this diversity ever vanished from New York, if the subway never rerouted or ran late again, it would no longer be New York.

![New York City subway](images/021.jpg)

**The Moments I'm Tired of New York**

Too many to count. Most of them involve the subway or traffic:

- Taking a cab in Manhattan? Most of the time, walking is faster than sitting in gridlock.

- The vast majority of New York subway stations have no elevators. Excuse me? The subway is over a hundred years old — just be grateful it runs at all. But parents hauling strollers up and down stairs, tourists dragging suitcases up steps — their souls are quietly crumbling.

- If you see an unusually empty subway car, don't celebrate too soon. Check first whether it smells like a homeless person has been living in it. I once stepped into a car reeking so badly I nearly threw up my New Year's Eve dinner.

- In New York, high heels live in your bag. You wear flats to walk. A few blocks on the sidewalk and a good chunk of the heel is already worn down. Shoe repairs are expensive — fix them a few times and you might as well buy a new pair.

- Have you ever been in New York in winter, when the snow on the streets starts to melt? The filth is layered — slush upon slush, mixed with garbage. Miss a step and you could plunge knee-deep into a hidden snow pit.

- Oh, and I almost forgot: terrorist threats, big and small, are normal. Don't make a fuss. Just check whether the subway is delayed today — that's what really affects your commute.

There is no better way to start the day than: **"The subway wasn't delayed today."**
