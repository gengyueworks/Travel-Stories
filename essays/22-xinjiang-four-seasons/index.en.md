**This June, we went to Xinjiang. It was a journey destined to be unforgettable. If not for this trip, perhaps our paths would never have crossed.**

**@Yue**

![Yue](images/003.jpg)

-

Photographer, journalist, travel writer.

An idealist with a childlike heart, a practitioner who works with sincerity and substance.

In June, I published a piece titled *I'm Going to Yili to Photograph Lavender — Will You Come?*

I have flown thousands of miles over the years, yet Xinjiang is the place where I was born and raised. **"Wander across ten thousand rivers and mountains — there is always one place you call home."**

The article was shared only as a preview in my circle of friends, and almost overnight, most of the spots were filled. The trip was ready to go.

But just before departure, I received an important photography assignment in Morocco. I had to miss this journey through Northern Xinjiang, much to my regret. The people who signed up had an incredible time. This is a story of those brought together by fate. To have connected you all — that brings me genuine joy.

**@Jinxiu**

![Jinxiu](images/004.jpg)

-

Beijing. Global traveler, soon to study in the UK.

"We came from all corners of the world — different jobs, different backgrounds, different tastes in food, different personalities and circles. If not for Xinjiang, we might have gone our whole lives without ever crossing paths, or passed each other by and forgotten. But it was precisely this group of such different people, gathered together, that gave off an astonishing light. **We seemed different on the surface, but deep down we were startlingly similar.** We were around the same age. We each had our own pursuits in life — different, yet pursued with the same tenacity. Our values aligned. We all seemed fiercely individual, yet equally soft at heart. All of this, all of it, carried us forward together, inseparable. As Sister Jing put it: feet in the mud, hearts toward the sun — that fits every one of us."

**@Eileen.C**

![Eileen.C](images/005.jpg)

-

From Beijing. Works for an airline and travel company. Amateur screenwriter, lover of theater, film, and art.

"Passing through the magnificent Guozi Valley Bridge, riding horses on the grasslands of Sayram Lake, experiencing the pastoral life of the Kalajun Grasslands — great felt yurts, sugar pastries, mare's milk wine, large baked naan, fragrant milk tea. **Braving the risk of melting to feel the sun that Monkey King once endured at the Flaming Mountains, a fireball overhead, yellow sand underfoot, baking in the sun for five hours just for a photograph — that kind of grit.** The scenery along the way, and the companions by my side — all of it, worth treasuring."

**@Linlin**

![Linlin](images/006.jpg)

-

Runs a clothing shop in Zhengzhou. A wonderful cook.

"Kalajun was one of the destinations I had most looked forward to in Xinjiang. The unexpected mishaps that day left me with a real regret about the scenery. Bayanbulak Grasslands

**Walking through the vast desert**

This is the desert closest to any city in the world, known as "the desert within the city." It is also the last sacred ground that interprets the disappearance of the ancient Loulan Kingdom, formed during the Han Dynasty. With a fireball overhead and yellow sand beneath our feet, we baked in the desert for five hours — fighting for the perfect photograph!
